# The research cell and the research coordinator: what they do each day, week and month

Written 2026-09-30 for the clarity audit (docs/ux/22). It extends
`research-cell.md` (the 14 jobs and the decision each drives) with the rhythm
of the work: what the first desk opens the system for in the morning, what it
does on a set day of the week, and what it owes at month end. Every view in
`docs/audit/research-cell/` is judged against this list.

## What the research (retrieved 2026-09-30) says

- **Indexing is checked at the source, not on the journal's own site.** A
  research office confirms the exact title, ISSN and publisher, then checks
  Scopus Sources, the Web of Science Master Journal List or DOAJ, and that
  coverage is current ([journalmetrics.org](https://www.journalmetrics.org/blog/how-to-verify-journal-indexing)).
  This is job 1 and 2 of the cell.
- **The old safety net is gone.** UGC stopped maintaining the UGC-CARE list on
  11 February 2025 and now points institutions to "suggestive parameters"
  with Scopus and Web of Science as the benchmark, and asks each institution to
  keep its own committee list ([Gurjas](https://gurjas.org/insights/ugc-care-discontinued/)).
  That is why the college keeps its own journal watch-list, with a reason,
  rather than trusting an outside list to stay current.
- **Incentive schemes in Indian colleges follow one pattern:** the claimant
  gives proof of publication, the office scrutinises it (only affiliated
  authors qualify; several authors share the amount), an approver signs, and
  the registrar or accounts pays ([Crescent scheme](https://bsacist.crescent.education/research/about-us/research-incentive-scheme/),
  [KIET](https://www.old.kiet.edu/incentive-for-researchers)). The chain here
  is the same, with the Director added.
- **Turnaround targets:** none of the schemes found publishes one. This college
  sets its own: a claim should be decided within 14 days (the service level in
  the system) and, as an aim, within a week. The system already reports
  "within a week" in the monthly report.

## The cell (research supervisor and colleagues) at the first desk

### Every day (open the system once in the morning, once after lunch)
1. **See what is mine and what is late.** The claims given to me come first,
   then the oldest. Anything past 14 days, or that will be tomorrow, is
   today's first work.
2. **Decide claims.** Open each claim in the review workspace, compare it with
   the record and the PDFs, then clear it, send it back with reasons, hold it or
   refuse it. Job 1 to 4, 6 and 14 of `research-cell.md`.
3. **See what came back.** A claimant who fixed a sent-back claim, or a claim
   the Principal returned with a reason, needs a second look; it should not
   hide in the same list as brand-new claims.
4. **Answer the question a flag raises.** New flags (from the file check or a
   colleague) are answered with a reason that the next reader can rely on.
5. **Answer a faculty member** who asks why their claim is where it is.

### Every week
6. **Clear the tail.** The ageing view: what is in 8 to 14 days and will breach
   next week; chase or reassign.
7. **Batch the clean ones.** Clear all the claims that pass every check together.
8. **Go through open flags** and close the ones that have been answered;
   escalate flags on claims already paid.
9. **Look at the journals waiting.** A journal on the watch-list with claims
   waiting is looked at before those claims are cleared.

### Every month
10. **Report the month** to the Principal: received, cleared, sent back, not
    accepted, median days, within a week, per person. The system produces it;
    the cell checks it and downloads the CSV.
11. **Refresh the journal lists.** After the monthly Scopus run, look at what
    was discontinued and add doubtful venues to the watch-list with the reason.
12. **Go back over the archive** for NAAC/NIRF: is every cleared paper in the
    record, are imported claims with a zero amount explained.

## The coordinator (owns the desk's flow and two scheme rules)

### Every day
1. **See the desk's health in one glance:** waiting, not given to anyone, past
   14 days, on hold.
2. **Give the new claims to reviewers** (the balance of who has how many), and
   take a claim back from someone who is away.

### Every week
3. **Workload and pace:** who holds how many, who cleared how many this week,
   is the queue growing (came in against decided).
4. **Chase what is stuck** at the Principal, Director and Finance desks. Counts
   only, in line with the role rules.
5. **Final-year project teams:** one claim per team, by the mentor; which
   teams are not claimed and which mentors have no account.

### Every month and every year
6. **Monthly processing report** (same as the cell's) for the Principal.
7. **Research faculty and their quota** at the start of the year: who is
   research faculty, what the threshold is, that "paper k of N" is right.
8. **Watch-list review:** which journals can come off, which should go on.

## What each view must answer (the test for the audit)

| View | The question, in the person's words | The answer at the top |
|---|---|---|
| Home | What do I do first today? | Mine and late first; today's target; what came back |
| Flags | Which doubts are still open, and what did we answer before? | Open by kind; on paid claims; resolve with a reason; history |
| Past claims | Was this ever claimed or paid, and what happened? | Find by claim number, title, DOI, person; outcome in a word |
| Journals | Which venues do we doubt, and which claims does that touch? | Watched journals, why, claims affected |
| Coordination: Desk | Is the desk on time, and who holds what? | Waiting, not given, late, on hold |
| Coordination: Monthly report | What do I tell the Principal? | The month in six figures and a download |
| Coordination: Research | Are the scheme rules being met? | Teams claimed, mentors missing, watch-list size |
